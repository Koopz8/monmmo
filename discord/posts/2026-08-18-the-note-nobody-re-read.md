**Our robot player has never read a single sign in the game.** Not one, in the
whole life of this project.

It wasn't skipping them. The record it walks the world with — the same record the
server uses — simply never carried signs at all. **519 of them**, invisible to
everything.

They're in now. What did they turn out to be worth? **Almost exactly nothing.**
Not one extra square of the map opened up. Seven switches flipped, which comes to
roughly two people becoming visible somewhere. Months of content nothing could
see, worth two villagers — and we only know that because we measured it instead
of assuming a find that big had to matter.

They did break something. **Two of the switches that signs touch get turned back
off again**, so the walk no longer settles down — it goes round in a loop now, at
five of six settings. That's the first thing in this project a run can *undo*,
and it's a better problem than the one it replaced.

**Before that, a fortnight spent checking our own notes.** There's a summary
sheet at the top of the working notes that every session reads first. It went in
as a single block **thirty-nine rounds of work ago and nobody ever re-ran a line
of it.** Several were wrong — one had been corrected in the very message that
announced the correction, and the wrong version sat there anyway, because writing
something down isn't the same act as fixing what people actually read.

Worse: four of its figures were numbers **no tool here can produce.** They had
the shape of measurements and nothing could ever have contradicted them.

**And the most confident finding of that stretch was mostly an artefact.** We'd
published "27 numbers being used two different ways at once — sixteen times what
chance would give you." Counted properly, 26 were ordinary numbers that happened
to match. The real answer is **one**, which is exactly what chance predicts.

Nothing the game does changed. What we believe about it is a lot more earned.
**3,067 tests.**
